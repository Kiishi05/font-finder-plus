# Font Finder Plus wins on breadth and privacy

Font Finder Plus has no single rival that does what it does. Each feature has a competitor, but they are spread across a dozen tools. **WhatFont (about 3M Chrome users) and Fonts Ninja (about 900K) own the "hover to name a font" habit.** FontSwap by FontAlternatives is the closest match to "Make it free". The open-source getfont copies the swap, axis and tokens set. A crowd of new token extractors covers exports. WhatTheFont and WhatFontIs own image identification. No tool found combines page inspection, honest licences, language coverage and **image identification that runs on your own device**. That combination is the reason to choose Font Finder Plus. Competitors are ahead on reach, catalogue size, image accuracy on commercial fonts, ready-made Figma import and some paid depth. On Figma, html.to.design imports a page in two ways: its servers render a pasted URL, or its Chrome extension captures the page in your own browser and hands it over by file or clipboard. The Figma Plugin API supports almost all of your companion plugin plan. The one thing it lacks is a direct link from a Chrome extension, so the hand-off should be a clipboard paste. Of your planned additions, the Figma plugin, multi-font and font-name detection in social images, Adobe Fonts links, the health check and the Edge build are clear wins. The timeline needs careful permission design. A Firefox build is real work, and "Do I have this installed" cannot work there.

## Read this first: many numbers are not confirmed

The research environment could not open the Chrome Web Store, chrome-stats.com, figma.com, help.figma.com, developers.figma.com, html.to.design, divriots.com, fonts.ninja, whatfontis.com, helpx.adobe.com or fontalternatives.com. Every figure from those sites came from search result summaries, not from reading the live page. User counts, ratings, prices, version dates and plan limits in this report are therefore marked **(to verify)**. Several sources are vendor comparison pages or SEO roundups. "Not found" in this report means "not found in the sources we could reach". It does not prove a feature is absent. The Chrome Web Store long tail holds many small "AI font identifier" listings that could not be inspected.

## Twelve tools split Font Finder Plus's feature list between them

### Hover identifiers own the install base

**WhatFont** has about **3,000,000 Chrome users** and a rating near **4.0 from about 2.1K ratings** (to verify) ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/whatfont/jabopobgcpjmedljpbcaablpmlmfcogm?hl=en)). Its last confirmed update was version 3.2.0 in March 2024, though one roundup mentions a later 3.3.0 (to verify) ([lilachbullock.com](https://www.lilachbullock.com/best-font-finder-chrome-extensions/)). It names the font and its styles, and little more. It is free on Chrome and Safari ([getwhatfont.com](https://getwhatfont.com/)).

**Fonts Ninja** has about **900,000 Chrome users** and claims 1M+ across browsers, with a rating around **4.4** (to verify) ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fonts-ninja/eljapbgkmlngdpckoiiibecpemleclhh); [fonts.ninja](https://fonts.ninja/tools/font-identifier-benchmark)). It shows name, styles, foundry and price. It claims its "Font DNA" reads the real font file, not just CSS. It suggests similar fonts, saves bookmarks to boards and sells trial installs through a desktop app ([Firefox listing](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/fonts-ninja/)). Identifying is free. **Bookmarking needs a free account.** Revenue comes from font sales and affiliate links ([fonts.ninja](https://fonts.ninja/tools/whatfont-alternative)). It runs on Chrome, Edge, Firefox (including Android) and Safari.

Smaller inspectors fill in detail. **Fontanello** (about 60K to 70K users, to verify) gives family, weight, size, colour and CSS on right-click and collects no data ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fontanello/jdlhfjlpaijjhklfadlhbbmpjfddkglc); [privacy policy](https://fontanello.app/privacy-policy)). **CSS Peeper** is a wider design inspector. Its free tier allows 3 "smart inspections" a day. Ultra costs **$4.99 a month** billed yearly and adds typography hierarchy analysis (to verify) ([csspeeper.com/pricing](https://csspeeper.com/pricing)). Chrome DevTools also shows the real rendered font, including fallbacks when a webfont fails ([umaar.com](https://umaar.com/dev-tips/230-rendered-fonts/)).

### Free-alternative tools are small but aimed straight at you

**FontSwap by FontAlternatives** is the closest rival to "Make it free". It detects every font on a page using canvas checks of the real rendered font. It flags premium fonts, suggests free Google Fonts ranked by visual similarity, and has a Replace button for live swapping. It is free with no premium tier and runs only in the tab ([FontSwap page](https://fontalternatives.com/fontswap/); [privacy](https://fontalternatives.com/privacy/)). Its catalogue is limited. The site lists **about 377 premium fonts and 153 free fonts** ([llms.txt](https://fontalternatives.com/llms.txt)). Its scores mix x-height, width and contrast with editorial judgement, for example **GT Alpina to Source Serif Pro at 84%** ([GT Alpina](https://fontalternatives.com/alternatives/gt-alpina/)). No user count or rating was found. Whole-page swap was not confirmed.

**getfont by pretnicx** is open source and MV3. It inspects fonts, swaps them live, tweaks variable axes, finds similar fonts from 300+ Google Fonts, and exports Tailwind, SCSS and TS tokens. It has no telemetry. It is installed by "Load unpacked", so it has no store reach ([GitHub](https://github.com/pretnicx/getfont)). **Snapfont** previews Google, local or uploaded fonts on a live site in Chrome, Firefox and Edge ([getsnapfont.com](https://getsnapfont.com/)).

### Token extractors make exports table stakes

In 2025 and 2026 a wave of extensions began exporting a site's typography. **Design System Extractor** exports W3C DTCG JSON, CSS custom properties and Tailwind config ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/nnjekphemngphnhkacefcnobblojapgc)). Design Token Extractor, Typography Extractor, Yoink, Font of Web and DesignDNA do similar jobs. TokenSnap charges for anything beyond JSON ([TokenSnap](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ficdicoikbpldomappjjdpmnjmilmjne)). None showed a direct Framer or Webflow export. None showed scale ratio detection. The field is crowded and has no leader.

### Image identifiers all upload your image

**WhatTheFont** (MyFonts, Monotype) matches against **over 200K font styles** on Monotype's servers ([Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/fr/node/6519)). Results link to the MyFonts store. Its mobile apps score only **about 2.2 to 2.9 stars**, with crash reports from February 2025 (to verify) ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/android/whatthefont/com.monotype.whatthefont?country=us)). **WhatFontIs** is server-side. PRO costs **$49.99 a year** (to verify) ([whatfontis.com/about](https://whatfontis.com/about.html)). Its Chrome extension draws complaints that capture opens a separate black page and identifies nothing ([Chrome Web Store reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/font-identifier-by-whatfo/ncjkkfbddfkkemiejpfdgjcifjmbcloc/reviews)). **Fontspring Matcherator** lets you correct the detected letters, which is the closest public match to your letter correction ([Fontspring](https://fontspring.com/matcher)). **Adobe Fonts visual search** and **Adobe Capture** match only Adobe's catalogue and need an Adobe account ([Adobe HelpX](https://helpx.adobe.com/fonts/using/visual-search-adobe-fonts.html)). **Mixfont Lens** is an open-weight model that aims at free font matches. It is the closest idea to your Google Fonts matching and could in principle run locally ([Mixfont](https://www.mixfont.com/models/lens)).

## Privacy, honesty and breadth are why people would switch

The strongest claim no rival can make is **"your image never leaves your device"**. Every major image identifier uploads the picture and matches against its own sales catalogue. Font Finder Plus runs OCR and matching locally against about 1,900 Google Fonts plus the fonts on the current page. **Matching against the page's own fonts has no equivalent anywhere.** Only DOM tools know page fonts, and they do not read images ([makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/fontofweb-review/)).

"Local and no account" alone is not unique. Fontanello, FontSwap and getfont make the same promise. What sets you apart is the stack around it. Licences are the clearest case. No mainstream tool reads a detected font and gives a per-font, plain-language licence summary inside the browser. What exists is static explainers, enterprise risk tools such as Extensis, and file-upload metadata readers such as JAD ([Extensis](https://www.extensis.com/font-risk-assessment-tool); [JAD](https://jad-apps-site.pages.dev/font-tools/guides/font-metadata-licensing-compliance)). Users are confused here. One guide warns that **"finding a name does not grant permission to use a commercial font"** ([macmyths](https://macmyths.com/how-to-identify-a-font-in-an-image-6-reliable-methods/)). Your rule of never inventing terms answers that directly.

Matching quality is the next reason. FontSwap ranks a fixed list of about 377 premium fonts. Your match % comes from metrics measured on whatever font the page loads. That covers obscure foundry fonts too, provided accuracy holds. Language coverage packs (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Arabic, Vietnamese) and a glyph inspector with Unicode names appeared in no competitor. Neither did "Do I have this installed", compare two tabs, weights loaded versus used, live pairings on real page content, or PNG share cards. Pairing sites such as Fontjoy and Fontpair preview on placeholder text, not on your page ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/fontjoy/pricing); [SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com/compare-font-pair-vs-typewolf)).

Finally, you answer the most common complaints. **WhatFont reviews in 2026 say it stopped working on newer Chrome and fails on local HTML files** ([Chrome Web Store reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/whatfont/jabopobgcpjmedljpbcaablpmlmfcogm/reviews)). A Fonts Ninja reviewer lost access to similar fonts after an update ([Extpose](https://extpose.com/ext/40851)). Another says it "can't even save most fonts" ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/eljapbgkmlngdpckoiiibecpemleclhh)). CSS Peeper users dislike a donation popup and dispute a geolocation call on each page load ([Chrome Web Store reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/css-peeper/mbnbehikldjhnfehhnaidhjhoofhpehk/reviews)). Image tools need very clear images and push paid fonts ([retouchinglabs](https://retouchinglabs.com/?p=58255)). Your in-page image editor, local library and free, uncapped use answer each of these.

## Competitors lead on reach, catalogue and Figma import

**Reach is the biggest gap.** WhatFont and Fonts Ninja have millions of installs and years of reviews. Fonts Ninja is on four browsers, including Firefox for Android and Safari through a Mac app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fonts-ninja/id1480227114)). Font Finder Plus is Chrome only today.

**Catalogue size matters for images.** WhatTheFont searches over 200K styles. You search about 1,900 Google Fonts plus page fonts. For a commercial font in a photo, you will usually return the closest free alternative, not the exact font. That is fine if the UI says so plainly. It is a weakness if it looks like an exact answer.

**Image accuracy is unproven on both sides.** No independent 2025 to 2026 head-to-head benchmark was found. Research models report very high accuracy, such as **99.0% top-1** on one paper's own benchmark, but that is not a product ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13889)). Fonts Ninja publishes a self-run benchmark on 200 websites as marketing ([fonts.ninja](https://fonts.ninja/tools/font-identifier-benchmark)). You have no public number yet.

**Fonts Ninja can install trial fonts** through its desktop app, which an extension cannot do. **CSS Peeper Ultra** offers "locate typography instances" and hierarchy analysis ([csspeeper.com/pricing](https://csspeeper.com/pricing)). **Stark** previews typography fixes in the page and flags text under 12px ([Stark](https://www.getstark.co/support/getting-started/using-typography/)). **Onylogy Font Checker** runs 8 readability checks including the 45 to 75 character measure ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ampafojcccmfaoelojmgdpllkmjeiblj)). Compare your readability check against those two. **Firefox DevTools** already has a variable axis editor, so developers there have a free baseline ([devtoolstips.org](https://devtoolstips.org/tips/en/list-used-fonts/)).

**Figma import is owned by others.** html.to.design, Builder.io and now Figma itself capture web pages into Figma. You should not compete on that. You should fix what they leave behind, as the next section shows.

## html.to.design renders pages on its servers or in your browser

html.to.design, made by ‹div›RIOTS, is a Figma plugin plus a Chrome extension. It takes three inputs: a URL, pasted HTML and CSS, or a capture from the extension ([html.to.design docs](https://html.to.design/docs/what-is-html-to-design/)). For a URL you paste the link in the plugin and click Import ([divriots blog](https://divriots.com/blog/introducing-html-to-design)). The page must be rendered somewhere, and the plugin cannot do it in Figma's sandbox. The privacy policy lists submitted URLs as "Input Data" processed to build the design ([privacy policy](https://divriots.com/legal/html.to.design/privacy)). URL imports also hit "human verification" walls that the extension avoids ([bot-protected sites](https://html.to.design/docs/bot-protected-sites)). Together these point to a headless browser on ‹div›RIOTS servers. That last step is an inference, as the engine is not public.

The extension captures the page exactly as you see it, including pages behind a log-in and local sites. It hands the capture over as a **.h2d file** you drag into the plugin, sends it to the plugin, or **copies it to the clipboard** so it pastes straight into Figma ([divriots, 600K users post](https://divriots.com/blog/html-to-design-extension-reaches-600k-users)). Import options build auto layout, local styles and variables, hover states as components and links ([import options](https://html.to.design/docs/import-options/)). The free tier gives **10 imports every 30 days**, though another doc page says 12 (to verify) ([Free features](https://html.to.design/docs/free-features)). The extension has about **600K users** (to verify) ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/htmltodesign/ldnheaepmnmbjjjahokphckbpgciiaed)).

**Its font handling is name matching only.** It shows a "Download missing fonts" button. You can map a missing font to one you have, and it reuses that mapping next time. Its own docs tell you to check Chrome DevTools > Rendered Fonts when names do not match ([Missing fonts](https://html.to.design/docs/missing-fonts)). There is no licence, no preview of the missing face and no similarity-based stand-in. That is exactly your opening.

The market is also shifting. **Figma bought ‹div›RIOTS' web-to-design technology** for Figma Make. html.to.design stays independently run ([divriots](https://divriots.com/blog/new-figma-partnership-to-bring-figma-make-to-the-canvas)). Figma now captures external websites with its own Chrome extension, and running apps through the MCP tool `generate_figma_design`. Both produce **flat layers not linked to libraries** ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/40826832449303-Turn-webpages-into-editable-design-layers)). One third-party post says the capture is in beta and limited to paid plans (to verify) ([toknow.ai](https://toknow.ai/posts/figma-capture-webpage-editable-design-layers/)). No source shows Figma detecting fonts, licences or stand-ins. **So yes, a Figma plugin can import a website, but only with a server or a browser extension doing the rendering.** A plugin-side URL renderer would duplicate free and native tools. Pair with them instead: the user captures the page with Figma or html.to.design, then Font Finder Plus fixes the fonts and builds the styles.

## The Figma Plugin API covers the companion plugin, except the hand-off

Each piece of your plan maps to a known API. Doc pages were read through search summaries only, so check wording before building.

**Receiving the scan.** No source shows a Chrome extension talking directly to a Figma plugin. The plugin UI is a sandboxed iframe with a null origin ([Creating UI](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/creating-ui); [forum](https://forum.figma.com/t/plugin-dev-postmessage-not-working-the-target-origin-provided-null-does-not-match-the-recipient-windows-origin/87747)). Every major tool uses a file, the clipboard or its own server. For a server-free product, **the clipboard is the best fit**: the extension copies a JSON payload and the user pastes it into the plugin. A downloaded file is the fallback.

**Showing installed versus missing fonts.** `listAvailableFontsAsync` returns the fonts the user can use: installed, Google and org-shared ([typings](https://wild.figma.com/plugin-docs/api/typings/); [forum](https://forum.figma.com/ask-the-community-7/loading-fonts-in-a-plugin-25747)). Compare the scan against that list. `hasMissingFont` flags text nodes with missing fonts ([Working with Text](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/working-with-text/)). Do not test by loading. Loading a missing font fails, and some reports say `loadFontAsync` can hang, so wrap it in a timeout ([forum](https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/loadfontasync-stuck-indefinitely-43195)). Two traps matter. **Figma refreshes its Google Fonts list only about quarterly**, so a new family on Google may be missing in Figma ([forum](https://forum.figma.com/ask-the-community-7/can-you-update-the-google-fonts-please-42331)). Shared fonts exist only on Organization and Enterprise plans ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403175325719)). Whether Adobe Fonts activated through Creative Cloud appear in the list is unconfirmed.

**Building text styles and variables.** Create styles with `createTextStyle`. Bind variables with `TextStyle.setBoundVariable` ([docs](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/api/properties/TextStyle-setboundvariable)). Bindable fields are font family, size, style, weight, letter spacing, line height, paragraph spacing and paragraph indent ([VariableBindableTextField](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/api/VariableBindableTextField)). Use STRING variables with the FONT_FAMILY and FONT_STYLE scopes and FLOAT variables for sizes ([Update 91](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/updates/2024/04/16/version-1-update-91)). Figma has no single "typography" variable, so each field gets its own ([atomize.tools](https://atomize.tools/blog/figma-typography-variables/)). Text styles take weight as a style name such as "Bold", not a number ([Tokens Studio](https://docs.tokens.studio/token-types/token-type-font-weight)). If the real font is missing, set the style to the stand-in and keep the real family in a variable or description, so one click can swap it back later. Since September 2026, `loadFontAsync({ family })` can load every style of a family at once ([Update 138](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/updates/2026/09/03/version-1-update-138/)).

**Previewing missing fonts.** **Plugins cannot outline text** the way the editor can ([forum](https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/how-do-i-outline-text-through-the-plugin-52771)). The workaround is to parse the font file in the plugin UI with a library such as opentype.js, build glyph paths for the sample text, and place them with `figma.createNodeFromSvg` ([typings mirror](https://pub.dev/documentation/typings/latest/d_figma_plugin_typings/PluginAPI$Typings/createNodeFromSvg.html)). No source confirmed this end to end, so prototype it first. Outlines also avoid re-hosting the font file, which matters for licences.

**Explaining licence and source, and suggesting stand-ins.** These are plain UI in the plugin, fed by data the extension already has. Fetching font files from site CDNs inside the plugin would need `allowedDomains: ["*"]` with a written justification ([manifest docs](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/manifest)). Servers must also send permissive CORS headers because of the null origin ([forum](https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/cors-error-in-figma-plugin-despite-configuring-alloweddomains-and-devalloweddomains-36708)). It is simpler to send the needed data, or pre-built outlines, inside the pasted payload.

**Charging later.** The Payments API supports one-time and subscription plugins with trials. Paid plugins must keep some free features ([figma.payments](https://developers.figma.com/docs/plugins/api/figma-payments); [Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/12067637274519)).

Existing font plugins work only inside the file. Font Fixer, Quick Replace Font (about 5.3K users, to verify) and Font Scanner find and replace fonts already in a document ([Font Fixer](https://www.figma.com/community/plugin/1635657955765400482/font-fixer); [Quick Replace Font](https://www.figma.com/community/plugin/1241949869279607046/quick-replace-font)). **Replace Fonts, Font Sense** suggests the closest match by category, weight range and usage, the nearest thing to your stand-ins ([Font Sense](https://www.figma.com/community/plugin/1607033265662222323/replace-fonts-font-sense)). Type System Builder ($5) and TypeFlow generate styles bound to variables ([Gumroad](https://brandingboard.gumroad.com/l/type-system-builder-figma-plugin); [TypeFlow](https://jennakang0077.github.io/typeflow-landing/)). None starts from a live website, and none shows licences or measured metric matches. Figma's own missing-font dialog sometimes fails to appear on imported files, with no bulk fix as of 2024 ([forum](https://forum.figma.com/t/missing-font-dialog-doesnt-show-up/72192)). That is a real pain your plugin can solve.

## Five planned additions are clear wins, three need care

| Planned addition | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Figma companion plugin | Build first | Fills the font gap left by html.to.design and Figma's own capture. API supports it. Use clipboard hand-off. |
| Adobe Fonts links | Build, cheap | Kits load from use.typekit.net, so detection is easy. Deep link format is unverified, so fall back to search. |
| Multi-font and font names in social images | Build, unique | No tool saves every font in an image at once, or reads printed font names. |
| Type system health check | Build | Rivals each do one slice. A combined score is new. |
| Edge version | Build, near free | Same MV3, side panel and Local Font Access. Mostly a listing job. |
| "Fonts I've seen" timeline | Build with care | Only one small rival does it, and it was flagged for risky permissions. |
| Framer and Webflow exports | Partial | Framer CSS is fine. Webflow paste format is unofficial and fragile. |
| Firefox version | Later | Real porting work. "Do I have this installed" cannot work. |

**Adobe Fonts links.** Adobe web kits load from `use.typekit.net/<kitId>.css`, with `use.typekit.com` on older sites ([Adobe HelpX](https://helpx.adobe.com/pt/fonts/web/web-design-and-development/use-typekit-net.html); [embed codes](https://helpx.adobe.com/tr/fonts/using/embed-codes.html)). Parse the kit CSS to get family slugs. A link of the form `fonts.adobe.com/fonts/<slug>` is plausible but not documented, so test it and fall back to an Adobe Fonts search link. These links also help image results: when the closest free match is not the real font, an Adobe link gives the honest next step.

**Multi-font and font names in social images.** "Multi-font" elsewhere means picking one region at a time. WhatTheFont spots several fonts but still asks you to choose one ([It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/news/monotype-whatthefont-031117)). Fontspring tells you to crop or split the image ([Fontspring](https://fontspring.com/matcher)). Adobe asks for a single line ([Adobe HelpX](https://helpx.adobe.com/fonts/using/visual-search-adobe-fonts.html)). **No tool found reads printed font names** such as "Font: Clash Display" and links them to a catalogue. You already have local OCR. Fuzzy-match the words against Google Fonts names plus a list of known commercial names with Adobe links, then offer "Add all to collection". This serves the "10 fonts you should use" post that is common on social media.

**Type system health check.** Stark, Onylogy, TypeFlux and WCAG Mini Check each test one slice: small text, line length, line height or spacing ([Stark](https://www.getstark.co/support/getting-started/using-typography/); [Onylogy](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ampafojcccmfaoelojmgdpllkmjeiblj); [WCAG Mini Check](https://chromeboard.com/extension/wcag-mini-check-hfppaamdobpaokaloilmgeaiphifbneb)). No widely used combined score was found. You already have the parts: type scale, weights loaded versus used, readability and failed fonts. Add scale consistency, number of families and weights, and font-display. Show what each fix saves.

**"Fonts I've seen" timeline.** Only one extension, FontFinder, keeps a local, site-grouped history you can search and export. A scanner flags it as having **"high-risk permissions"** ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fontfinder/ncembeiepbfcphhiplbbonejobohbhlb); [extscope](https://extscope.org/extension/ncembeiepbfcphhiplbbonejobohbhlb)). A passive log needs all-sites access, which costs trust. Make it opt-in, local only and pausable per site. A lighter default is to log only pages where the user opened Font Finder Plus, which needs no extra permission. Including image detections in the timeline would be new.

**Framer and Webflow exports.** Framer Text Styles hold font, size, weight, line height and colour, and custom fonts upload as WOFF or WOFF2 ([Framer help](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-to-add-custom-fonts/); [text styles](https://www.framer.com/ja/help/articles/how-to-use-text-styles/)). There is no public paste format for styles, so a Framer plugin is the stronger long-term route. Whether its API can create text styles is unverified. Webflow's `@webflow/XscpData` clipboard format is undocumented and can change at any time ([Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/where-to-find-the-details-on-webflow-xscpdata-type/286508)). Ship a Webflow variables list with clear setup steps instead. Also note that Webflow's Google and Adobe Fonts integrations do not expose variable axes, and custom font files are capped at 4MB ([Webflow help](https://help.webflow.com/lesson/variable-fonts); [custom fonts](https://university.webflow.com/lesson/custom-fonts)).

**Edge and Firefox.** Edge supports the same MV3 side panel and Local Font Access ([Edge API support](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/extensions/developer-guide/api-support); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Local_Font_Access_API)). An Edge Add-ons listing is mostly about being found. Firefox needs a `sidebar_action` instead of the side panel, user-granted host access, and no `queryLocalFonts` at all, so **"Do I have this installed" must be hidden or replaced** there ([MDN sidebar_action](https://developer.mozilla.org/Add-ons/WebExtensions/manifest.json/sidebar_action)). A framework such as WXT can share the side panel code across both ([WXT](https://wxt.dev/entrypoints/sidepanel)). Fonts Ninja's Firefox presence shows the audience exists. Do it after the Figma plugin.

## Ten further ideas come from gaps and complaints

**Publish an honest accuracy benchmark.** Nobody has an independent one. Run your page detection and image matching on a fixed public set, show top-1 and top-5, and repeat it with each release. This counters Fonts Ninja's vendor benchmark and builds trust in your match %.

**Label image results as "exact" or "closest free".** Your catalogue is about 1,900 Google Fonts. Say plainly when the real font is probably commercial, then offer Adobe and Fontspring links. Users already complain that matches are "similar, not exact" and "not free" ([retouchinglabs](https://retouchinglabs.com/?p=58255)).

**Auto-split images into text blocks and match each one.** WhatTheFont, Adobe and Fontspring all force one region per lookup. A "match every block, save all" flow beats them all.

**Show the scale ratio.** No extractor was found that names the ratio, such as "Major Third, 1.25". Add it to the type scale extractor and the health check.

**Read the licence fields in the font file.** JAD shows the name table holds licence text and a licence URL ([JAD](https://jad-apps-site.pages.dev/font-tools/guides/font-metadata-licensing-compliance)). Show them verbatim beside your summary. Also warn about common foundry rules. Fontfabric, for example, forbids conversion, which affects subsetting, and desktop licences rarely cover web use ([Fontfabric](https://www.fontfabric.com/fontfabric-licensing/)).

**Show where each icon font and webfont loads from.** A Font Detector user asked for exactly this ([Extpose](https://extpose.com/ext/205259/en-US)). It also feeds licence and Adobe kit detection.

**Make reliability a feature.** WhatFont's biggest complaints are breaking on new Chrome and failing on local files. Support `file://` pages behind a clear toggle. Explain in the panel why a page cannot be scanned, such as chrome:// pages and the Web Store ([frontend-hero](https://frontend-hero.com/best-font-identifier-chrome-extensions)).

**Lead with a light permission story.** Use activeTab by default and ask for all-sites access only for features that need it. Fontanello asks for all-sites access at install ([AMO](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/fontanello/)). CSS Peeper's privacy dispute shows how much users care.

**Check stand-ins against Figma's font list.** Because Figma's Google Fonts list lags by months, prefer stand-ins that `listAvailableFontsAsync` actually returns. Otherwise your suggestion becomes another missing font.

**Add a "fix a captured page" mode in the plugin.** Scan the frames Figma's capture or html.to.design produced, find their missing fonts, and offer previews, licences and stand-ins in one pass. This turns the commoditised import market into your distribution channel.

## Conclusion

The competitive picture shifts the product question. Font Finder Plus does not need to win "what font is this?". WhatFont and Fonts Ninja hold that with installs you cannot match soon. It needs to own **"what can I legally use, and how do I get it into my design?"** Every rival stops before that point. Hover tools stop at the name. Image tools stop at a store link. Figma importers stop at a "download missing fonts" button. Your local processing, honest licences and measured stand-ins are the parts of that answer nobody else gives.

That makes the Figma plugin more than an add-on. Web-to-Figma capture is now native and free, so many designers will soon hold Figma files full of missing fonts. The plugin turns that wave into demand for Font Finder Plus. Build it on a clipboard hand-off, prove your matching with a public benchmark, and verify the "to verify" figures on the live store and vendor pages before you use any of them in marketing.
